Once the shape of a room is settled, what remains is the texture of its surfaces. Diffusers scatter incident sound rather than reflecting it specularly, and their performance is governed by the topology of the surface itself — the depth, width and sequence of its wells — at a scale far below that of the room. This research parameterised that topology and searched it against scattering and diffusion coefficients, replacing the catalogue of standard diffuser types with surfaces designed for the specific space they belong to.

The workflow couples parametric models directly to acoustic simulation so that a change in the surface returns a scattering prediction in seconds instead of hours, which is what makes an optimization loop — and an architectural conversation about the surface — possible at all. Numerical predictions were compared against scale-model and laboratory measurements to establish where the simulated coefficients can be trusted.
